LESSON board
TITLE The chessboard
CHAPTER First steps
SUMMARY 64 squares, files and ranks.
PAGE
FEN 8/8/8/8/8/8/8/8 w - - 0 1
HIGHLIGHT yellow h1
TEXT Welcome to chess! The chessboard has 64 squares: 32 light and 32 dark ones.
TEXT |Always put the board so that the square in the bottom right corner is a light square. Remember: "light on the right!"
PAGE
FEN 8/8/8/8/8/8/8/8 w - - 0 1
HIGHLIGHT green a1 a2 a3 a4 a5 a6 a7 a8
HIGHLIGHT blue b1 c1 d1 e1 f1 g1 h1
TEXT The lines of squares going up the board are called FILES. They have letters: a, b, c, d, e, f, g and h.
TEXT |The rows going across are called RANKS. They have numbers from 1 to 8.
TEXT |The green squares are the a-file, the blue squares (with a1) are the first rank.
PAGE
FEN start
HIGHLIGHT yellow d1 d8
TEXT This is how the game begins. White's pieces are on ranks 1 and 2, Black's on ranks 7 and 8.
TEXT |White always makes the first move. Look at the queens: the white queen stands on a light square and the black queen on a dark square - each queen on her own colour.
END

LESSON notation
TITLE Chess notation
CHAPTER First steps
SUMMARY How squares and moves are written.
PAGE
FEN start
HIGHLIGHT green e4
HIGHLIGHT blue g1
TEXT Every square has a name: first the file letter, then the rank number. The green square is e4. The blue square, where a knight stands, is g1.
PAGE
FEN start
ARROW g1f3
TEXT Moves are written with a capital letter for the piece: K = king, Q = queen, R = rook, B = bishop, N = knight. Pawns have no letter.
TEXT |"Nf3" means: a knight moves to f3. "e4" means: a pawn moves to e4.
TEXT |An "x" means a capture (Bxe5), "+" means check, "#" means checkmate, "O-O" is castling on the king's side and "O-O-O" castling on the queen's side.
TASK
FEN start
TEXT Now you try! Play the move e4: the pawn in front of the king moves two squares forward.
GOAL move e4
HINT Click the pawn on e2, then click the square e4.
SUCCESS Well done! 1.e4 is the most popular first move in chess.
WRONG That is a legal move, but not e4. Find the pawn on e2!
END

TEXT The KING moves one square in any direction: forwards, backwards, sideways or diagonally.
TEXT |The king is the most important piece. If your king is trapped (checkmate), you lose the game. That is why a king may never move to a square where it could be captured.

GOAL move Kxd5
HINT The pawn is right in front of your king.
SUCCESS Yes! The king captured the pawn, because nobody was protecting it.
END

LESSON capture
TITLE Capturing and piece values
CHAPTER First steps
SUMMARY Which pieces are worth more?
PAGE
FEN start
TEXT When a piece moves to a square with an enemy piece, it captures it. The captured piece leaves the board.
TEXT |Pieces have different values (in pawns): pawn 1, knight 3, bishop 3, rook 5, queen 9. The king cannot be captured - it is priceless!
TEXT |Try to capture pieces worth more than the ones you give away.

GOAL move Nxd5
HINT Which squares can the knight on c3 jump to?
SUCCESS Fantastic! You won the queen - worth 9 pawns - with a knight.
END

SUCCESS Blocked! The bishop shields your king.
FEEDBACK Kf1 Moving the king also gets you out of check - but this time, try blocking with the bishop.
FEEDBACK Kd1 Moving the king also gets you out of check - but this time, try blocking with the bishop.
FEEDBACK Kf2 Moving the king also gets you out of check - but this time, try blocking with the bishop.
FEEDBACK Kd2 Moving the king also gets you out of check - but this time, try blocking with the bishop.
TASK

LESSON checkmate
TITLE Checkmate
CHAPTER First steps
SUMMARY How a chess game is won.
PAGE
FEN R5k1/5ppp/8/8/8/8/8/6K1 b - - 0 1
HIGHLIGHT red g8
ARROW red a8g8
TEXT CHECKMATE means: the king is in check and there is NO way out - it cannot move, the check cannot be blocked and the attacker cannot be captured.
TEXT |Checkmate ends the game. The player who gives checkmate wins!
TASK
FEN 6k1/5ppp/8/8/8/8/8/R5K1 w - - 0 1
TEXT The black king is hiding behind its pawns. Give checkmate in one move!
GOAL mate
HINT The pawns block the king's escape squares. Attack along the back rank!
SUCCESS Checkmate! This is called a "back-rank mate".
TASK
FEN k7/8/1K6/8/8/8/7Q/8 w - - 0 1
TEXT Your king and queen work together. Find the checkmate!
GOAL mate
HINT Your king already guards the squares a7 and b7.
SUCCESS Checkmate! The king and queen make a great team.
END

LESSON stalemate
TITLE Stalemate
CHAPTER First steps
SUMMARY No move, but not in check: a draw!
PAGE
FEN 7k/5Q2/6K1/8/8/8/8/8 b - - 0 1
HIGHLIGHT yellow h8
TEXT Here it is Black's turn. The black king is NOT in check, but every square it could go to is attacked, and Black has no other pieces.
TEXT |This is STALEMATE, and stalemate is a DRAW. Be careful when you are winning: always leave the other king a move, unless you give checkmate!
TASK
FEN 7k/8/4Q1K1/8/8/8/8/8 w - - 0 1
TEXT Give checkmate - but watch out for stalemate!
GOAL mate
HINT Give check along the back rank.
SUCCESS Checkmate! You avoided the stalemate trap.
FEEDBACK Qf7 Careful! After Qf7 Black cannot move but is not in check: that is stalemate, a draw. Try again.
WRONG That is not checkmate yet. Look for a check the king cannot escape.
END

LESSON en-passant
TITLE En passant
CHAPTER First steps
SUMMARY The special pawn capture.
PAGE
FEN 8/8/8/3pP3/8/8/8/8 w - - 0 1
ARROW e5d6
HIGHLIGHT red d5
TEXT A special rule: if a pawn moves two squares and lands right beside an enemy pawn, the enemy pawn may capture it "in passing" - EN PASSANT in French - as if it had moved only one square.
TEXT |This is only allowed on the very next move!
TASK
FEN 4k3/8/8/3pP3/8/8/8/4K3 w - d6 0 1
TEXT Black has just moved the pawn from d7 to d5. Capture it en passant!
GOAL move exd6
HINT Your e5-pawn goes diagonally to d6, and the pawn on d5 disappears.
SUCCESS Yes! That was en passant - you now know every special rule of chess.
END

LESSON draws
TITLE Draws
CHAPTER First steps
SUMMARY When nobody wins.
PAGE
FEN 8/8/4k3/8/8/3K4/8/8 w - - 0 1
TEXT A game can also end in a DRAW (nobody wins):|- STALEMATE: the player to move has no legal move but is not in check.|- NOT ENOUGH PIECES to checkmate, for example king against king.|- AGREEMENT: both players agree to a draw.
TEXT |- REPETITION: when the same position appears three times, the player to move may CLAIM a draw.|- FIFTY MOVES without any capture or pawn move: a draw may be claimed.
TEXT |In Beginner Chess you can claim with the "Claim Draw" button. After five repetitions or 75 such moves the game is drawn automatically.
END
